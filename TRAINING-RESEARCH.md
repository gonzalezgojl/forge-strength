# FORGE — Training research notes

This summary explains the evidence used to shape the current template. The programme is a practical starting point for a healthy adult seeking muscle growth and measurable strength progress with four weekly sessions, one lower-body resistance session and additional cycling. It is not a medical prescription or a claim that one routine is universally optimal.

## 1. ACSM position stand (2026)

Currier BS et al. *Resistance Training Prescription for Muscle Function, Hypertrophy, and Physical Performance in Healthy Adults: An Overview of Reviews.* Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise. 2026;58(4):851–872. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1249/MSS.0000000000003897

This overview synthesised 137 systematic reviews and more than 30,000 participants. Higher weekly training volumes (around 10 or more sets per muscle group per week) were associated with greater hypertrophy. For strength, heavier loads (at least 80% 1RM), full range of motion, 2–3 sets per exercise, placing priority lifts early and training at least twice weekly enhanced adaptation. The evidence did not show a consistent need to reach momentary failure on every set or to use one specific equipment type or periodisation model. The literature search extended through October 2024.

**FORGE application:** chest and back receive approximately 11 and 13 main-target sets; priority compound lifts are placed first. Lower-body volume is deliberately more conservative because this plan includes one leg day and additional cycling.

## 2. Weekly volume and frequency (2025)

*The Resistance Training Dose Response: Meta-Regressions Exploring the Effects of Weekly Volume and Frequency on Muscle Hypertrophy and Strength Gains.* PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41343037/

This meta-regression included 67 studies and 2,058 participants. More weekly sets were associated with greater muscle-size and strength gains, but with diminishing returns. Once volume was considered, the independent frequency effect on hypertrophy was less clear; frequency showed a clearer relationship with strength. The analysis distinguished direct from indirect work and found fractional counting of indirect sets fit the evidence best.

**FORGE application:** the volume map reports main-target sets rather than counting every compound set as a full set for all assisting muscles. Three upper sessions distribute work; they are not claimed to be intrinsically superior when weekly volume is equal.

## 3. Supersets and time efficiency (2025)

Zhang X et al. *Superset Versus Traditional Resistance Training Prescriptions: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis Exploring Acute and Chronic Effects on Mechanical, Metabolic, and Perceptual Variables.* Sports Medicine. 2025;55:953–975. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-025-02176-8

The review included 19 studies and 313 participants. Supersets generally reduced session duration while maintaining a similar number of repetitions and volume load, with broadly similar chronic strength and hypertrophy outcomes. They also increased perceived exertion. Antagonist pairings were better suited to retaining work output than pairing two exercises that fatigue the same movement pattern.

**FORGE application:** supersets are reserved for accessory pairings (biceps/triceps) and a non-competing hip-thrust/calf pairing. Heavy bench, row, squat and hinge work remains in straight sets.

## 4. Inter-set rest (2024)

Singer A et al. *Give it a rest: a systematic review with Bayesian meta-analysis on the effect of inter-set rest interval duration on muscle hypertrophy.* Frontiers in Sports and Active Living. 2024;6:1429789. DOI: https://doi.org/10.3389/fspor.2024.1429789

The meta-analysis included nine studies. It suggested a small benefit to resting longer than 60 seconds rather than 60 seconds or less, potentially because longer rests preserve training volume. It did not identify a clear additional hypertrophy benefit from resting more than 90 seconds versus 90 seconds or less, but uncertainty remained.

**FORGE application:** 2.5–3 minutes for priority heavy compounds, around 2 minutes for secondary compounds and 60–90 seconds for isolations. These are practical targets; rest longer when needed to maintain repetitions and technique.

## 5. Exercise variation (2022)

Kassiano W et al. *Does Varying Resistance Exercises Promote Superior Muscle Hypertrophy and Strength Gains? A Systematic Review.* Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research. 2022;36(6):1753–1762. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1519/JSC.0000000000004258

The review identified eight studies with 241 participants (all young men). It suggested systematic variation may help regional hypertrophy and dynamic strength, whereas random or excessive variation and redundant choices may hinder progress. The small, demographically narrow evidence base limits certainty.

**FORGE application:** grip, angle, stability and loading profile vary across the week, but exercises remain stable through a training block so progress can be measured. The suggested 6–8 week review is a coaching framework, not a biologically fixed duration.

## 6. Proximity to failure (2024)

*Exploring the Dose-Response Relationship Between Estimated Resistance Training Proximity to Failure, Strength Gain, and Muscle Hypertrophy: A Series of Meta-Regressions.* PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38970765/

The analysis found a clearer relationship between sets being closer to failure and hypertrophy than with strength. RIR estimates were inferred from study descriptions and the authors described the models as exploratory, so exact optimal RIR targets remain uncertain.

**FORGE application:** about 2 repetitions in reserve (RIR) on compound lifts and 1–2 RIR on isolation work. RIR 2 means an estimate that two more technically sound reps could be completed. Failure on every set is not required.

## Limitations and adjustment

- Research does not define a single perfect routine for everyone. This programme balances the stated time, cycling and four-day constraints with the evidence above.
- One lower-body day has less weekly frequency than a two-day lower split. It follows the preference for one dedicated leg day; if lower-body development becomes a higher priority, some work could be distributed into a second brief exposure if schedule and recovery permit.
- If performance declines across multiple sessions, soreness persists, cycling quality drops or joints become irritated, reduce accessory sets or add recovery before increasing volume.
- Use a pain-free range of motion and seek qualified clinical guidance for persistent pain, injury or relevant medical conditions.
