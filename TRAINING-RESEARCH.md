# FORGE — Training research notes

This is the evidence summary used to shape the current training template. The programme is a practical starting point for a healthy adult seeking muscle growth with measurable strength progress, four weekly sessions, one lower-body resistance session and additional cycling. It is not a medical prescription or a claim that one routine is universally optimal.

## 1. ACSM position stand (2026)

Currier BS et al. *Resistance Training Prescription for Muscle Function, Hypertrophy, and Physical Performance in Healthy Adults: An Overview of Reviews.* Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise. 2026;58(4):851–872. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1249/MSS.0000000000003897

The overview synthesised 137 systematic reviews and more than 30,000 participants. It found that higher weekly training volumes (around 10 or more sets per muscle group per week) were associated with greater hypertrophy, with a dose-response relationship. For strength, heavier loads (at least 80% 1RM), full range of motion, 2–3 sets per exercise, beginning sessions with priority lifts and training at least twice weekly enhanced adaptation. For hypertrophy, the included evidence did not show a consistent requirement to train to momentary failure, or to use one specific equipment type, periodisation model or set structure. The review's literature search extended through October 2024.

**FORGE application:** chest and back receive approximately 11 and 13 main-target sets, respectively; priority compounds are placed first; the weekly split spreads upper-body work across three sessions. The lower-body volume is deliberately more conservative because the user prefers one leg day and also cycles.

## 2. Weekly volume and frequency (2025)

*The Resistance Training Dose Response: Meta-Regressions Exploring the Effects of Weekly Volume and Frequency on Muscle Hypertrophy and Strength Gains.* PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41343037/

This meta-regression included 67 studies and 2,058 participants. More weekly sets were associated with greater muscle-size and strength gains, but with diminishing returns. Once training volume was considered, the evidence for frequency independently improving hypertrophy was less clear; frequency showed a clearer relationship with strength. The authors also distinguished direct from indirect sets and found that counting indirect work fractionally best fit the evidence.

**FORGE application:** the volume map shows main-target sets rather than pretending every compound set belongs fully to every assisting muscle. Upper-body frequency is used to distribute manageable amounts of work, not because three sessions are inherently superior when weekly volume is equal.

## 3. Supersets and time efficiency (2025)

Zhang X et al. *Superset Versus Traditional Resistance Training Prescriptions: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis Exploring Acute and Chronic Effects on Mechanical, Metabolic, and Perceptual Variables.* Sports Medicine. 2025;55:953–975. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-025-02176-8

The review included 19 studies and 313 participants. Supersets generally reduced session duration while maintaining a similar total number of repetitions and volume load, with broadly similar chronic strength and hypertrophy adaptations. They also raised perceived exertion and internal load. Antagonist pairings were better suited to retaining work output than supersets that fatigue the same movement pattern.

**FORGE application:** supersets are reserved for smaller accessory pairings (biceps/triceps) and a non-competing hip-thrust/calf pairing. The main bench, row, squat and hinge work remains in straight sets to protect quality.

## 4. Inter-set rest (2024)

Singer A et al. *Give it a rest: a systematic review with Bayesian meta-analysis on the effect of inter-set rest interval duration on muscle hypertrophy.* Frontiers in Sports and Active Living. 2024;6:1429789. DOI: https://doi.org/10.3389/fspor.2024.1429789

The meta-analysis included nine studies. It suggested a small benefit to resting longer than 60 seconds rather than 60 seconds or less, potentially because longer rests help preserve training volume. The evidence did not identify a clear additional hypertrophy benefit from resting more than 90 seconds compared with 90 seconds or less, although uncertainty remained.

**FORGE application:** 2.5–3 minutes for priority heavy compounds, around 2 minutes for secondary compounds, and 60–90 seconds for isolations. These are practical ranges; take longer when needed to maintain the target repetitions and technique.

## 5. Exercise variation (2022)

Kassiano W et al. *Does Varying Resistance Exercises Promote Superior Muscle Hypertrophy and Strength Gains? A Systematic Review.* Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research. 2022;36(6):1753–1762. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1519/JSC.0000000000004258

The review identified eight studies with 241 participants (all young men). It suggested that systematic variation can help regional hypertrophy and dynamic strength, whereas random or excessive variation and redundant exercise choices may impair progress. The small and demographically narrow evidence base limits certainty.

**FORGE application:** the programme varies grip, angle, stability and loading profile across the week, but retains each exercise for a training block so progression can be tracked. Variation is not increased merely for novelty; the exercise menu is also kept practical for an ordinary commercial gym. The suggested 6–8 week review point is a coaching framework, not a biologically fixed duration.

## 6. Proximity to failure (2024)

*Exploring the Dose-Response Relationship Between Estimated Resistance Training Proximity to Failure, Strength Gain, and Muscle Hypertrophy: A Series of Meta-Regressions.* PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38970765/

The analysis found a clearer relationship between sets being closer to failure and hypertrophy than with strength. Its RIR estimates were derived from study descriptions and the authors described the models as exploratory, so exact optimal RIR values remain uncertain.

**FORGE application:** around 2 repetitions in reserve (RIR) on compounds and 1–2 RIR on isolations. RIR 2 means you estimate that two more technically sound repetitions could be completed. Reaching failure on every set is not required.

## Limits and how to adjust

- The published evidence does not identify a single perfect routine for every person. The current programme balances the stated time, cycling and four-day constraints with the evidence above.
- The lower-body day has less total weekly frequency than a two-day lower split. It is designed around the user's preference; if lower-body growth becomes a priority and recovery/time allow, distributing some work into a second brief leg exposure could be considered. The programme also adds only two direct abdominal sets and two calf sets; increase either gradually only if these are explicit priorities and recovery permits.
- If performance declines across multiple sessions, soreness persists, cycling quality drops, or joints become irritated, reduce accessory sets first or add recovery before increasing volume.
- Use a pain-free range of motion and seek qualified clinical guidance for persistent pain, injury or relevant medical conditions.
